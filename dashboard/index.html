Two shocks, pulling in different directions

Any price has two separate properties. How expensive it is on average, and how much the 36 states and the FCT disagree with each other. A national average only reports the first.

Each dot below is one fuel in one episode. The further right, the more the price level rose. The further up, the more the states came apart. The two episodes land in completely different places.

Petrol came apart, then snapped back

The shaded band is the distance between the cheapest and the dearest state in that month. The line through it is the national median.

Watch the band, not the line. Through early 2025 the median barely moves while the band opens to four times its usual width, then closes again inside a single month. Switch to another fuel and nothing like it happens.

Did any of it reach food prices?

For four months the six regions were paying very different amounts for the same petrol. If fuel costs push food prices up, the regions that paid most should show the biggest food rises afterwards.

They do not. The region that paid a third more than anyone else has one of the smallest food increases, and the two regions where food rose most sit at opposite ends of the fuel range. With six regions this rules out a large effect and cannot rule out a small one.
